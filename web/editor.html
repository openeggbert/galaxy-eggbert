3D World Editor

Build and change worlds inside the game: fly a free camera, aim at cells, place the game's own blocks, objects and scenery, then play-test at once.

The editor follows the editors of Speedy Blupi and Eggbert 2: the same palette groups in the same order, the same variant cycling, the same automatic re-tiling of rock edges, the same lift speeds. Because it works in 3D it adds tools of its own: a free-fly camera, 3D box selection, copy and paste, quarter turns, backups and thumbnails. This page describes the state as of 2026-11-10.

The rule. The editor is the one approved exception to the faithful-remake rule: it may add authoring UX, but no gameplay mechanics. Everything it places is a block, object or decor the game already has, behaving exactly as in the campaign.

The world screen

Each of the three players has ten world slots, shown as a grid of five by two. An occupied slot opens; an empty one creates a new world there, a small playable starter (a 3x3 board of rock, a treasure chest, an exit arrow and Blupi's start). A slot's bin deletes its world after a second click. Slot 1 is seeded once with the showcase world, and the gallery (every block, object and scenery piece in one world) goes into the next free slot. Worlds outside the ten slots and shared worlds are listed under "Other worlds": a click copies one into a free slot. Back (or Escape) returns to the main menu.

Editing a campaign world: xnjttjpo

Typed during play (no Enter), the cheat opens the editor on the mission's own world file. Saving writes the shipped world itself, the versions before it kept in customworlds/campaign-backups/; Stop returns to the mission, started again with the edits. Afterwards Pause shows an Original button: after "Sure?" the world as shipped comes back and the mission restarts. On the web the shipped worlds live in the page's data, so each edit is kept in the browser's storage and laid over them at every start. Like every typed cheat it needs a build with cheats enabled and is blocked by fairplay; see Cheats & Diagnostics.

Camera and aiming

The camera flies freely: W/A/S/D move, Ctrl rises, Shift or Z descends, the right mouse button held turns the view, the mouse wheel zooms. A ray from the cursor through the voxel grid finds the aimed cell (VoxelRaycast):

  • the cyan outline is where Place will add a block, beside the aimed face;
  • the red outline and tint mark the edit target, the thing that Turn, Variant and Remove act on, with its coordinates in the status line;
  • in an empty world the ray falls onto the ground plane, so the first block can be placed.

NumPad 4/6 (X), 8/2 (Z) and 7/9 (Y) move the placement cell away from the aimed one; that coordinate stays fixed while the camera moves, until NumPad 5 aims with the cursor again. The world's bounds carry its axes: X red, Y green, Z blue.

The palette

The icon palette has the ten groups of Eggbert 2's editor in its order, then one group of Galaxy's own. A group mixes terrain blocks, objects and big scenery (BigDecor) as the original's menus did. An entry the game cannot place yet stays in its original position and shows "Not yet implemented."

Placing and changing

  • Place: left click, Space or the PLACE button puts the selected entry in the cyan cell.
  • Variants: clicking again on the cell just placed cycles through its variants; Ctrl+click places without cycling (and picks a random member of a tree, palm or house family). V cycles the aimed block through its variant family, or through the edge pieces of its re-tiling family.
  • Re-tiling: placing or removing a block re-tiles it and its four neighbours, as the original's AdaptBorder does, so rock and slime edges follow what borders them.
  • Turn (O): a quarter turn of the red target. A plate, fan or wind tile changes its axis; a window, sign or saw faces another side; models and Blupi starts turn too.
  • Remove: Delete, Backspace or the middle button removes the red selection.
  • One exit: placing a red exit arrow or the final golden key replaces the previous exit, as in the original editor.

3D selection, box fill, copy and paste

  • Shift + NumPad (or Shift + arrows, Home, Page Up) extends an inclusive 3D selection from the aimed cell, its size shown while selecting. Place fills it with the selected block, Remove clears it, Escape cancels. The whole fill is one Undo.
  • F on two corners fills the box between them.
  • C on two corners copies a box: its blocks, their turns, and the objects and BigDecor anchored in it. P turns pasting on and off; Q turns the copy a quarter, X mirrors it; a click stamps it with its corner at the preview (Ctrl+click keeps what lies under its empty cells). One Undo takes a stamp back. The copy is kept across the worlds of one session.

Undo and redo (U and R) cover every edit: blocks, objects, BigDecor, sky, music, Blupi starts, turns, object ends and timings.

Objects, routes and lifts

A lift or creature from the moving mechanisms is placed, then the next click sets the end of its route (the editor prompts for it); Escape or a click on it again leaves it still. The four lift entries are one lift at the original PC editor's four speeds, in 20 Hz ticks per cell: slow 20/20 with a rest of 20, normal 10/10/10, fast 6/6/3, and fast-up/slow-back 5/22/2.

  • G picks an object or a Blupi start; T sets the picked object's end; Tab steps through its timing fields (advance, recede, rest at the start, rest at the end) and +/- change the value; Delete removes the object.
  • Routes are drawn as numbered open cubes, 1 at the start and 2 at the end, for every lift, fish, moving mine, bird, wasp, creature and enemy Blupi whose ends differ. Opaque terrain hides them.
  • Lifts move along their routes in the editor, with their waits and return trip, by the same routine as in play. This preview is never saved into the world.

The four Blupi starts

Characters and goals holds four independent starts: yellow, orange, blue and green, each drawn in its colour. Moving, turning or removing one leaves the others alone, even when two share a cell. Yellow is the single-player start, and its heading is the one Blupi faces when the world begins.

Sky region and music

The Background group's thumbnails choose the world's sky region (its background picture); the Left and Right arrows step through them too. Its note entries, or the M key, choose the world's music: none or one of the original ten tracks.

Saving and play-testing

  • Enter (or Tools > Save, a gamepad's Start) saves. The first save after a world was opened keeps the file as it was as a backup, three generations deep.
  • Every save also stores a 64x64 thumbnail of the editor's view, which the world screen shows on the slot.
  • The dice saves and starts a play-test; Pause's Back returns to the editor with the same world.
  • The Stop sign (F10) returns to the world screen. With unsaved changes it needs a second press.

Backups, export and import

On the world screen, Backups turns a click on a slot into a restore of its newest backup; clicking again steps further back and comes round to where it began. A deleted slot keeps its world the same way. Export turns a click on a slot into a copy in the shared folder (A-world-3.vwr for player A's slot 3, replacing an earlier export of that slot); on the web the file is also downloaded. Import opens the browser's file picker on the web; elsewhere it tells you where to put files. Shared worlds are listed first among the other worlds, newest first.

Touch

Touch editing needs no keyboard. The Move pad flies horizontally, the Up/Down buttons change height, and dragging the world with another finger looks around. Palette entries and PLACE are tapped as buttons. Tools > Select 3D replaces holding Shift, and six X-/X+/Y-/Y+/Z-/Z+ buttons extend the selection.

The menus and the world screen use the cursor automatically, so a whole session can start and end on a gamepad; the mouse keeps working while one is plugged in.

The Tools menu

For touch and gamepad, the Tools menu lists every command with its keyboard equivalent (EditorDeviceControls): Save (Enter), Undo (U), Redo (R), Select 3D, Cancel (Escape), Remove (Backspace), Turn (O), Variant (V), Pick object (G), Set end (T), Next field (Tab), Value + and Value -, Delete obj (Delete), Copy box (C), Paste (P), Turn copy (Q), Mirror (X), Box fill (F), Keep (Ctrl's place without cycling), Reset XYZ (NumPad 5), Music (M), Help (H) and Stop (F10).
