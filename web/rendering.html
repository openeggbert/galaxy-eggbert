Rendering

How a frame is drawn: the simulation is read into a SceneFrame draw list, WorldRenderer draws it, the HUD goes on top (as of 2026-11-10).

There is one renderer. It is built on CNA's XNA 4.0 graphics API with cna-engine's helpers (MeshBuilder, GpuMesh, TextureAtlas rectangles); see Engine Integration. Everything that changes how the game looks is presentation only: the simulation never reads anything back from the renderer. This page is the technical view; 3D Effects & Graphics describes what a player sees and sets.

A frame, step by step

GalaxyEggbertGame::DrawFrame() in src/GalaxyEggbertCNA/GalaxyEggbertGameDraw.cpp:

  1. Clear colour and depth to dark navy RGB(0, 35, 98), the corner colour of the menu pictures.
  2. Build the draw list: BuildSceneFrame() (src/GalaxyEggbertCNA/GalaxyEggbertGameScene.cpp). Skipped where no world is shown: the Wait and Init phases (unless the title scene is behind the menu), both Setup screens and the editor's slot browser.
  3. Shadow map (effects High and up): WorldRenderer::RenderShadowMap() draws the casters from the sun into their own targets.
  4. Ultra only: a geometry pass (WorldRenderer::DrawGeometry(): normal, depth, mirror per pixel) for the screen-space effects.
  5. World: DrawWorld() calls WorldRenderer::Draw(), into a render target when a finish (PostProcess or UltraFinish) is active, otherwise straight to the screen. The editor's gizmos follow, against the world's depth.
  6. Finish: bloom, grade and FXAA, or Ultra's HDR chain, onto the screen.
  7. 2D overlay: DrawOverlay() with depth testing off: underwater tint, HUD, touch pad, menus, notices.
  8. Captures: DrawFrameCaptures() (screenshots, editor thumbnails, autoplay shots, golden frames).

The game draws on CNA's 800 × 480 logical surface, which CNA scales to the window. Only Ultra draws the world at the window's own pixels; the HUD stays on the logical surface. The simulation steps 60 times a second; at the Setup's higher frame rates (120, 144, 240) the camera, Blupi, moving objects and particles are interpolated between steps.

The seam: SceneFrame

src/GalaxyEggbert/Game/SceneFrame.hpp is a plain draw list with no device, effect or GPU mesh in it. The host fills it from the simulation (which object shows, by which icon or which model pose, where); WorldRenderer draws only from it and from the loaded assets. The lists keep the order the world has always been drawn in, so blending and depth come out the same. It was introduced on 2026-11-09 as a pure refactor: golden frames, 1728 autoplay frames of 11 missions and 28 editor frames stayed byte-equal. (renderers.md once proposed two interchangeable renderers; the owner chose one.)

Inside WorldRenderer::Draw()

The order in src/GalaxyEggbert/Game/WorldRenderer.cpp, which matters for blending and depth:

  1. Fog on every world effect (effects on) or off again.
  2. The sky background.
  3. Terrain pass: reliefs updated near the camera's target, then the opaque terrain and lava; soft round shadows; lift and crate cubes; the grass overlay; Blupi and the vehicles; every object model; sign and spark decals; translucent models (a hidden or ghost Blupi); the editor's player starts; then the terrain's semi-transparent pass (water and animated tiles) after the models, so water covers what lies in it (OR-0052).
  4. Billboards, blended with NonPremultiplied (the sheets have straight alpha).
  5. Particles (effects on).

Sky background

Content/backgrounds/decorNNN.png is chosen by the world's sky region (28 of the 32 region ids have a picture; a missing one leaves the clear colour). It is drawn as one camera-facing quad 900 blocks away, inside the camera's 1000-block far plane. The 640 × 480 picture is wrapped round the horizon a whole number of times, so turning the camera scrolls it, and the camera's height moves it at 2/3 of the world's speed, as the original's parallax does (OR-0053).

Terrain

TerrainRenderer builds GPU meshes from the World, one cell per block, textured from the tile sheet through TileAtlas (a padded, mipmapped copy of object-m.png). How each block icon is drawn comes from BlockDefinitionRegistry: render modes UniformCube, DirectionalCube, InnerPillarBox, ThinBar, InnerFlatPlate, TripleCrossBillboard and SawBlade, with geometry helpers DirectionalCubeTiles, InnerPillarBoxTiles, ThinBarTiles and InnerFlatPlateTiles.

Patching instead of rebuilding

The world keeps a block revision and a bounded change journal (World::blockChangesSince()). GalaxyEggbertGame::RefreshTerrainMeshIfChanged() asks TerrainRenderer::PatchModelCells() to apply changes that only touch model-drawn cells (a clay bridge crumbling, saw switches); anything else, or unknown history, rebuilds the terrain, and the new renderer adopts the unchanged chunks' reliefs (AdoptReliefs()). Measured rebuilds dropped from 211 to 6 in world 065 and from 259 to 0 in world 094 (OR-0074).

Terrain reliefs

Tiles that have an original Ray Dream scene can show that scene's relief on their exposed faces near the camera (TerrainRelief.hpp, avatars3d/terrain/: 145 .gtr files serving 158 tile icons). The terrain is split into chunks of 8 × 8 × 8 cells; a chunk within 8 blocks of the camera's target is built as reliefs on worker threads (two at a time; on the web within the frame's budget) and released 4 blocks farther out; until then, and everywhere else, it is textured cubes. The reliefs are off by default: the Setup's "3D terrain" (terrainRelief=) turns them on, GALAXY_TERRAIN_RELIEF=0|1 and GALAXY_TERRAIN_RELIEF_RADIUS override (Configuration).

Brick edges and lighting

The brick wall's edge pieces 262, 263 and 283 are drawn ragged only at a wall's open end: four courses of bricks, each ending where its bricks end (BrickEdges.hpp, AppendRaggedBrick); elsewhere a whole cube. Collision stays the whole cell. Terrain, grass and the lift and crate cubes are lit by one directional light plus ambient (TerrainLighting.hpp: ambient 0.63, diffuse 0.42), so walls and steps no longer dissolve into one flat colour.

3D models

All models are CNA SkinnedModelEXT directories drawn with one SkinnedEffect (or the effects' model shader). They are converted from the original Ray Dream scenes (Assets & Tools).

  • Models in the terrain (BlockModels.hpp): the fans, the vanishing saucer, the crusher, the slime drip and the saw are animated models; still tiles such as the stone and white columns, the golden support, the barrel stack, the hanging bar, the teleporters, posts, the coloured doors and the spring are drawn as their scene's model when avatars3d/objects/ has it. Their roles and collision do not change; without the models they stay textured cubes.
  • Objects: ObjectModelTable reads icons.txt of each directory listed in avatars3d/objects/index.txt (121 models). The game picks an object's sprite icon exactly as the original would; an icon listed there is drawn as that model's pose (clip time, mirror, yaw, offset), any other icon stays a billboard.
  • Blupi (avatars3d/blupi/): 56 bones and one clip per BlupiController::AnimState (68 clips, 20 frames a second), with the face texture switched per frame from clips.txt. The vehicles (vehicles/helico, jeep, over, skate, tank) share his clip time and transform. Drawn in third person only; without avatars3d/blupi/ a fox placeholder (avatars3d/blupi_placeholder/) stands in.

Billboards, decals and the water

Whatever has no model is a camera-facing quad cut from its sprite sheet, rebuilt every frame. Explosions, splashes, sparks, the charge and the shield stay sprites by decision. Signs' painted faces and the dynamite and electrode sparks are decals generated from the sprites. Water is drawn as the outside of each water volume only, so a swimmer is not hidden behind stacked internal walls; under water the HUD lays a tint that fades in over the last 0.35 block below the surface (WaterViewImmersion).

Effect levels, technically

The Setup's "3D effects" (effectsLevel=0..4, off by default; GALAXY_EFFECTS=0-4|off|low|medium|high|ultra) sets SceneEffects. Shaders are GLSL 3.30 or GLSL ES 3.00 compiled at run time through CNA's ShaderEffect; where they do not compile, that part falls back to the stock effects by itself.

2D: HUD, menus and the editor's UI

Hud and InputPad draw real 3D quads with an orthographic projection (QuadBatch::FlushQuads), not SpriteBatch, so their order against the scene is explicit on every backend. They lay out in the original's 640 × 480 space, scaled by the viewport's height / 480 and centred; menu screens fill the side bars beside their pictures. Text uses Content/icons/text.png with the original's proportional glyph widths (TextMetrics.hpp). The editor's palette and toolbar use the same quad helpers in viewport pixels.

Golden frames: the rendering regression check

xvfb-run -a tools/verify_golden_frames.sh <build-dir>
xvfb-run -a tools/verify_golden_trace.sh <build-dir>

The first runs the game with --golden-capture (world 999, deterministic) and byte-compares frames 60, 120 and 180 with tests/golden/ (effects off), tests/golden/effects-medium/ and tests/golden/effects-high/. GOLDEN_UPDATE=1 rewrites only the effects' references; the classic ones are replaced by hand. Both need a display, so they are manual checks, not CTest cases. CTest covers the pure parts: VerifyTileFaces, VerifyTileCoverage, VerifyTerrainLighting, VerifyTileUvBounds, VerifyObjectModelTable, VerifyEffectParticles, VerifySceneEffects and VerifyUltra. See Testing.
